Frequently Asked Questions

Start with the questions most people have.

Every prospective team is different, but these answers explain the basic Golden Heart process and what to expect.

Where is Golden Heart located and who do you serve?

Golden Heart Service Dogs is based in Tulsa, Oklahoma. The program serves clients throughout Oklahoma, across the United States, and may consider international placements individually when the dog, handler, training plan, home environment, and transition logistics are an appropriate fit.

How much does a Golden Heart service dog program cost?

Current program ranges are Level One: $7,500–$10,500; Level Two: $12,500–$16,000; and Level Three: $18,000–$22,000. Final pricing depends on the individual dog, required task work, time involved, complexity, travel, and client needs.

What determines which service-dog level I need?

The level is not determined by diagnosis alone. Golden Heart considers the person's disability-related needs, required tasks, the dog's temperament and abilities, the amount and complexity of training, the environments where the dog will work, and the amount of handler education and transition support required.

What can be included in the program?

Depending on the individual contract, a program may include professional obedience, house manners, socialization, public-access preparation, environmental exposure, distraction work, task training, disability-specific service work, handler education, team training, transition training, real-world practice, appropriate veterinary care, and continued support after placement.

How do payments work?

Payment schedules are individualized and set out in the service agreement. Golden Heart typically uses several structures, including 50% at signing and 50% at completion; 100% at signing; 65% during the contract-signing month followed by monthly installments; or 50% at signing followed by monthly installments. Clients do not begin payments before the initial intake and documentation process has been reviewed and the client has been accepted.

Are payments refundable?

According to Golden Heart's current policy, once a dog is approximately halfway through its contracted program, payments are generally non-refundable because substantial training, veterinary care, food, equipment, and other expenses have already been invested. The signed contract controls the final refund and ownership terms.

How long does training take?

Training time varies significantly based on the dog's age, starting level, temperament, tasks, and the complexity of the client's needs. Programs generally involve several months to more than a year of professional training. Some dogs may require approximately 8–10 months or longer, and specialized placements can take a year or more. Graduation occurs when the dog and handler are appropriately prepared rather than on a fixed calendar date.

Will Golden Heart train a dog I already own?

No. Golden Heart does not currently train a client's existing dog through the service-dog program. The dogs are selected and provided through Golden Heart.

How does the pairing process work?

The current intake process includes two Zoom or FaceTime meetings, photos of the home inside and out, the most recent two months of bank statements for the individual receiving the dog, a description of the client's typical daily routine, preferred veterinarian and groomer information or reference, and a recommendation from an appropriate medical or mental-health provider. Additional information may be requested depending on the placement.

Should I upload bank statements or medical documents through the website form?

No. The website inquiry form is only for initial contact. Do not submit bank statements, medical records, provider letters, or other sensitive documents through the general website form. Golden Heart will explain how required documentation should be provided later in the intake process.

Do you work with long-distance or international clients?

Yes. Current travel fees are $3,500 within 500 miles of Tulsa and $4,200 within 900 miles. Beyond approximately 900 miles, travel and delivery arrangements are quoted individually. International placements may involve travel costs of approximately $5,500–$8,000 depending on destination and logistics. Travel arrangements and costs are discussed before the contract is finalized.

What happens if a placement is not working?

The first step is communication and evaluation. Golden Heart may provide additional training, transition assistance, troubleshooting, or a change in the training or placement plan. If the dog ultimately cannot remain with the intended handler, the situation is evaluated individually under the contract and with the dog's welfare and team safety in mind. Return, retraining, reassignment, or another appropriate placement may be considered.

What support is available after placement?

Continued support may include follow-up communication, training questions, troubleshooting, transition support, handler education, refresher guidance, assistance with changes in the dog's work, and additional training when appropriate. Basic support and guidance are part of the ongoing relationship; more extensive in-person refresher training, additional task training, or substantial retraining may be charged separately.

Does Golden Heart offer regular puppy or pet-obedience training?

No. Golden Heart's current public services are focused on service-dog programs. General puppy classes, pet obedience, off-leash pet-training, and therapy-dog programs are not currently offered as standalone public services.

Do you work with children or minors?

Yes. Golden Heart accepts pediatric placements, including autism-support placements, on an individual basis. A parent or legal guardian participates in the application, decision-making, training, and ongoing care. Children under age 13, and clients whose disability makes additional assistance necessary, will have a designated secondary handler.

Are medical-alert tasks guaranteed?

No specific medical-alert task is guaranteed for every dog. Medical alert and response work is individually evaluated and based on the dog's demonstrated abilities, temperament, training, and the handler's specific needs.

What happens when a service dog retires?

Retirement varies by dog and commonly occurs around 8–11 years of age depending on health, ability, and the work being performed. Golden Heart can help the handler plan for retirement and, when appropriate, a successor dog. In most cases, the retired dog may remain with the handler when that is in the dog's best interest and appropriate care can continue.

What are Golden Heart's business hours?

Messages are typically answered Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM–5:00 PM Central Time. Additional appointments may be requested outside regular business hours through direct message.

Does Golden Heart accept donations or sponsorships?

No. Golden Heart Service Dogs, LLC is a for-profit business and is not currently accepting charitable donations or sponsorships.
