Frequently Asked Questions

Start with the questions most people have.

These answers reflect the information currently provided for the Golden Heart website. Details can be expanded as policies and program specifics are finalized.

How do I apply for a service dog?

Golden Heart does not currently use a formal one-size-fits-all application. The process begins by contacting Golden Heart, followed by consultation, two Zoom/FaceTime meetings, and individualized information gathering.

What disabilities do you work with?

Golden Heart's provided service list includes PTSD, psychiatric disabilities, autism, medical alert needs, mobility assistance, and other qualifying disabilities. Eligibility and tasks are evaluated individually.

How much does a service dog cost?

Current program ranges are Level One: $7,500–$12,500; Level Two: $13,000–$16,500; and Level Three: $18,000–$22,000. Training scope and final program recommendations depend on individual needs.

How does the matching process work?

Placement is based on compatibility, lifestyle, disability-related needs, the dog's temperament, and the dog's abilities—not on diagnosis alone.

Do you work with out-of-state clients?

Yes, Golden Heart's current information includes a $3,500 travel fee within 500 miles and a $4,200 travel fee within 900 miles. These fees are intended to cover travel, training-related travel, hotel, and airfare expenses when applicable during the three-to-six-month working period. Details beyond 900 miles should be confirmed directly.

What happens after placement?

Golden Heart emphasizes continued support after placement, including follow-up training, troubleshooting, questions, refresher training, transition assistance, communication, and community resources.

Do you offer continued support?

Yes. "Forever Care" is intended to be a major part of the Golden Heart brand and program philosophy.

Do you train dogs I already own?

The materials provided so far do not specify an owner-dog service-dog training policy. Contact Golden Heart for a current answer.

How long does training take?

The provided information mentions a three-to-six-month timeframe when working with out-of-state clients, but it does not establish one universal training timeline for every service-dog team.

What is the difference between a service dog, therapy dog, and emotional support animal?

This is planned as an educational topic for the site. A full legal/educational explanation should be added with current jurisdiction-appropriate guidance before launch.

What documentation is required?

Golden Heart currently lists video meetings, home photos, veterinary and grooming references/referrals, a day-to-day routine example, recent bank statements, and a recommendation from a medical or mental-health professional. Sensitive records should be requested securely after initial contact rather than through a basic public web form.

What happens if the dog isn't the right match?

The materials emphasize matching based on compatibility and individual evaluation, but a specific rematching policy has not yet been provided. Golden Heart should publish the formal policy once confirmed.
