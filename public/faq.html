Frequently Asked Questions

Start with the questions most people have.

Every prospective team is different, but these answers explain the basic Golden Heart process and what to expect.

How do I get started with a service dog?

Golden Heart does not use a one-size-fits-all application. The process begins with an initial conversation, followed by consultation, two Zoom or FaceTime meetings, and individualized information gathering.

Where is Golden Heart located and who do you serve?

Golden Heart Service Dogs is based in the Tulsa, Oklahoma area. The program serves clients throughout Oklahoma and across the United States. Longer-distance and international placements may also be considered individually when the dog, handler, training plan, home environment, and transition logistics are an appropriate fit.

What disabilities do you work with?

Golden Heart works with qualifying needs that may include PTSD, psychiatric disabilities, autism support, medical alert or response needs, mobility assistance, and other disability-related needs evaluated individually.

How much does a service dog cost?

Current program ranges are Level One: $7,500–$12,500; Level Two: $13,000–$16,500; and Level Three: $18,000–$22,000. Final recommendations depend on the scope and complexity of the individual training plan.

How does the matching process work?

A dog is not matched by diagnosis alone. Golden Heart considers compatibility, lifestyle, daily routine, disability-related needs, home environment, the dog's temperament, and the dog's abilities.

Do you work with long-distance or international clients?

Yes. Golden Heart does not automatically exclude someone because they live more than 900 miles from Tulsa. Current travel fees are $3,500 within 500 miles and $4,200 within 900 miles. Beyond approximately 900 miles, travel is quoted individually. International travel may typically range about $5,500–$8,000 depending on distance and logistics, and is subject to change. Travel arrangements and costs are discussed before a contract is finalized.

What can a long-distance transition include?

Depending on the team and location, the plan may include service-dog delivery, travel to the client's location, in-person team training, a combination of in-person and remote follow-up, and additional transition or support arrangements.

Do you offer puppy or general obedience training?

No. Golden Heart currently focuses on service-dog training and occasionally therapy-dog training. General puppy training, pet obedience, advanced pet obedience, and off-leash pet-training programs are not offered as standalone services.

How long does training take?

Training timelines vary because each team and task plan is different. Out-of-state work may take place over a three-to-six-month timeframe, but there is no single universal timeline for every service-dog team.

What documentation may be requested?

Golden Heart may request two video meetings, photos of the inside and outside of the home, veterinary and grooming references or referrals, an example of the applicant's day-to-day routine, recent bank statements, and a recommendation from a medical or mental-health professional. Long-distance clients may also be asked for additional photos, videos, or information about the home and environment. Sensitive documents should not be sent through the general website inquiry form.

What happens after placement?

Golden Heart emphasizes continued support after placement, including follow-up training, troubleshooting, guidance, refresher training, transition assistance, continued communication, and community resources.

Do you accept donations or sponsorships?

No. Golden Heart Service Dogs, LLC is a for-profit business and is not currently accepting charitable donations or sponsorships.
