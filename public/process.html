The Golden Heart Process

From first conversation to forever care.

Golden Heart does not use a one-size-fits-all formal application. The process begins with conversation, evaluation, and careful information gathering.

The Golden Heart process

Get Started

Reach out with basic information about the prospective handler, location, needs, and goals.

Initial Review

Golden Heart reviews the inquiry and determines whether the program may be an appropriate fit.

Video Meetings

Two Zoom or FaceTime meetings help Golden Heart learn more about the prospective client, daily life, support needs, and expectations.

Pairing Evaluation

Home environment, routine, references, documentation, and disability-related needs are reviewed.

Dog Selection

A potential match is considered based on compatibility, temperament, abilities, environment, and required work.

Individual Training

Obedience, public access, task work, and environmental preparation are shaped around the future handler.

Client Preparation

The handler learns how to communicate with, care for, and work with the dog.

Placement & Transition

The team moves into real-life routines with support from Golden Heart.

Graduation

A milestone reached when the dog and handler are appropriately prepared.

Forever Care

Communication, troubleshooting, refresher guidance, and support continue after graduation.

Pairing requirements and document safety

Current pairing requirements

Golden Heart's pairing process is intentionally thorough so the placement can be evaluated for both the client and the dog.

  1. Two Zoom / FaceTime meetings
  2. Photos of the client's home, including inside and outside areas
  3. The most recent two months of bank statements for the individual receiving the dog
  4. A description or example of the client's typical daily routine
  5. Preferred veterinarian and groomer information or reference
  6. Recommendation from an appropriate medical or mental-health provider

Additional information may be requested depending on the individual placement.

Protecting sensitive information

Do not send bank statements, medical records, provider letters, or other sensitive documents through the general website inquiry form.

The initial inquiry is only the first step. If Golden Heart determines that the program may be an appropriate fit, the team can explain how required documentation should be provided securely during the intake process.

Golden Heart looks beyond diagnosis alone and considers lifestyle, environment, schedule, support system, expectations, and disability-related needs.

If a placement is struggling

Communication comes first.

If a placement is not working, Golden Heart begins with communication and evaluation. Depending on the circumstances, the team may provide additional training, transition assistance, troubleshooting, or a change in the training or placement plan.

If a dog cannot remain with the handler

The outcome is evaluated individually.

Golden Heart may determine that return, retraining, reassignment, or another appropriate placement is in the best interests of the dog and the working team. Exact rights and responsibilities are governed by the individual contract.

Forever Care

Graduation is a milestone—not goodbye.

Continued support may include follow-up communication, training questions, troubleshooting, transition support, handler education, refresher guidance, assistance with changes in the dog's work, and additional training when appropriate.

Basic support and guidance are part of Golden Heart's continued relationship with clients. More extensive in-person refresher training, additional task training, or substantial retraining may be charged separately depending on what is needed.

Forever Care also includes planning for the later stages of the partnership. Service-dog retirement commonly falls around ages 8–11 depending on the individual dog's health, ability, and work. Golden Heart can help handlers plan for retirement and, when appropriate, a successor dog. In most cases, the retired dog may remain with the handler when that is in the dog's best interest and appropriate care can continue.
