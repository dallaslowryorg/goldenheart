Service Dog Programs

Individual training for individual lives.

Golden Heart's service-dog program is built around compatibility, disability-related needs, lifestyle, temperament, and the skills a particular dog can reliably perform. Golden Heart is based in Tulsa, Oklahoma and works with clients throughout the United States, with international placements considered case by case.

Three program levels

Training scope grows with the complexity of the work.

Program ranges vary with the complexity of the training plan. Final recommendations depend on the individual team's needs, goals, and required task work.

How matching works

A dog is not selected by diagnosis alone. Golden Heart considers the prospective handler's lifestyle, daily routine, disability-related needs, home environment, compatibility, and the dog's temperament and abilities.

Eligibility and specific assistance tasks are evaluated individually. A listed service type does not guarantee that every dog or applicant will be an appropriate match.

Long-distance clients

Distance does not automatically rule out a placement.

Golden Heart is based in Tulsa, Oklahoma. Clients outside the local area are considered individually, and travel arrangements are discussed before a contract is finalized.

  • Within 500 miles of Tulsa: $3,500 travel fee
  • Within 900 miles of Tulsa: $4,200 travel fee
  • Beyond approximately 900 miles: individual quote based on logistics
  • International travel: typically about $5,500–$8,000, subject to change based on distance and travel requirements

Long-distance plans may include delivery of the service dog, travel to the client's location, in-person team training, remote follow-up, and additional transition support. Travel, transportation, lodging, airfare, and related costs may be added to the program.

International and longer-distance placements are evaluated case by case. Final travel arrangements and costs are confirmed before the agreement is finalized.

Additional information for long-distance clients

Golden Heart may request additional information before accepting a long-distance placement, including photos or videos of the home and environment, details about the client's daily routine, veterinary information, and other financial or program documentation.

Why the transition plan matters

The right plan depends on the dog, handler, disability-related needs, home environment, distance, and the amount of in-person training needed for a successful transition.
